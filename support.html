Frequently asked questions

How does redaction work, and why can’t it be undone?

While you are editing, a redaction is a layer you can move, adjust or delete. When you export, the app redraws the picture: the covered areas are painted as new pixels into a brand-new image, and the original pixels underneath them are simply not written into the exported file.

That is the point. A redaction that is only a sticker sitting on top of the original can be peeled off by anyone who opens the file in the right tool. Here the detail is gone from the exported copy, which also means it cannot be recovered from that copy — not by you, not by us. Keep the original if you may need it later.

The same applies to PDFs: the exported PDF is flattened, so the hidden text is not sitting underneath a black rectangle waiting to be selected and copied.

What’s the difference between blur and a black bar — which should I use?

They are not equally safe.

  • Black bar replaces the area with solid colour. Nothing of the original remains. This is the strongest option.
  • Mosaic at a high strength averages large blocks of pixels together and is also very hard to reverse.
  • Blur looks clean, but a light blur is a mathematical transformation that can sometimes be partially reversed, and short predictable strings such as a card number can in principle be guessed from a weak blur.

For documents, IDs, card numbers, account numbers and addresses, use a black bar or a strong mosaic. Blur is fine for a background, a face in a casual photo, or anything where a rough shape does not matter.

What does “Replace Original” do?

Exporting normally saves a new redacted file and leaves your original untouched. “Replace Original” instead asks Photos to replace that asset in your own photo library with the redacted version, so you do not end up with both copies on your phone.

Two things to know. First, Photos asks you to confirm the change — the app cannot do it silently. Second, Photos keeps the asset’s own record, so the original capture date and location stay attached to that library item, even though the redacted image itself carries no EXIF or GPS. If you do not want those kept, export a new copy instead and delete the original yourself.

Is anything uploaded? Do you see my photos?

No. There is no server, no account and no analytics in the app, so there is nowhere for your files to go. Face and text detection runs on your iPhone using Apple’s Vision framework, and speech transcription is set to on-device recognition in code, so audio is not sent to Apple’s servers for transcription either.

We never see your photos, documents or recordings. The only thing that leaves your phone is a file you deliberately share, and you choose where it goes.

The full detail is on the privacy policy.

How do I restore my purchase on a new phone?

Open the app, go to Settings and tap Restore Purchases. Make sure you are signed in to the same Apple ID that made the original purchase.

Because there is no account system, your purchase is tied to your Apple ID rather than to a login. Restoring works for the subscription and for the Lifetime purchase alike. If a restore does not bring Pro back, email us with the Apple ID region and the approximate purchase date and we will help.

How do I cancel my subscription?

Subscriptions are managed by Apple, not by us, so they are cancelled through your Apple ID:

  1. Open the Settings app on your iPhone.
  2. Tap your name at the top, then Subscriptions.
  3. Tap AI Censor and Redactor, then Cancel Subscription.

Cancel at least 24 hours before the current period ends, otherwise it renews for another period. After cancelling, Pro stays active until the end of the period you have already paid for. If you cancel during the 3-day free trial you are not charged. Refunds are handled by Apple at reportaproblem.apple.com.

Which iPhone and iOS version do I need?

AI Censor requires iOS 17 or later and is built for iPhone. On-device face and text detection and on-device speech transcription are provided by the system, so a newer iPhone will generally get through a large PDF or a long recording faster.

Automatic detection missed something. What now?

Treat automatic detection as a fast first pass, never as the final word. It can miss handwriting, unusual layouts, low-contrast or rotated text, a partly hidden face, or a number written in an unexpected format.

Always look over the result before sharing, and use Draw, Brush or Words to cover anything left over. Zooming in helps — a redaction that looks complete at a glance can leave a readable edge.

How do I report a bug or request a feature?

Email us. Use AI Censor Bug Report or AI Censor Feature Request as the subject so it lands in the right place.

For a bug, include your iPhone model, your iOS version, the app version from Settings, and the steps that reproduce it. Please do not send us the sensitive file itself — a description, or a screenshot with the sensitive parts already covered, is enough and is safer for you.
